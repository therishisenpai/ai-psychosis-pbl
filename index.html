AI Ethics & Cognitive Analysis

AI Psychosis

An analytical study examining cognitive, behavioral, and emotional deviations associated with excessive reliance on generative artificial intelligence systems, particularly among digitally native populations.

Introduction

Recent advancements in generative AI and large language models have enabled unprecedented levels of human–AI interaction. While these systems enhance productivity and accessibility, emerging literature suggests unintended psychological and cognitive consequences arising from excessive dependence.

Problem Statement

There is a lack of structured analytical frameworks to study psychosis-like behaviors—such as hallucination normalization, emotional dependency, and reduced independent cognition—observed in heavy users of generative AI systems.

Objectives

  • To study the phenomenon of AI-induced cognitive dependency
  • To analyze behavioral deviations linked to AI overreliance
  • To examine emotional attachment toward conversational AI systems
  • To discuss ethical, psychological, and societal implications

Methodology

This study follows a qualitative, literature-based analytical approach. Observations are derived from secondary research, academic publications, reports, and documented case studies related to AI dependency and cognitive offloading.

  • Literature review of peer-reviewed research
  • Comparative analysis of reported behavioral patterns
  • Conceptual classification of AI psychosis indicators
  • Ethical and cognitive impact assessment

Expected Outcomes

The study aims to provide a structured analytical framework for understanding AI psychosis, contributing insights into safer AI system design and responsible human–AI interaction.

Project Status

The project is currently in the research and documentation phase, with ongoing literature review and conceptual framework development.
